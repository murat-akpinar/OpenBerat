Sessions and access

A row is a session that would still authenticate — not a user who is active. The two read alike and only one of them is true. A session lives in Redis until it is signed out or it expires seven days later, and nothing requires anybody to still hold its cookie: a closed laptop, a cleared browser and a script that logged in and exited each leave one behind, working, for a week. That is the right thing to count here, because the question this page exists for is what would still let somebody in.

Read out of the index the kill switch uses, so it sees the user who signed in and opened nothing — the history tab never would. What it cannot tell you is when a session started or where from: the index holds a subject and its session keys, and nothing else. To end one, run the kill switch (INSTALL.md §6); nothing on this page writes.
